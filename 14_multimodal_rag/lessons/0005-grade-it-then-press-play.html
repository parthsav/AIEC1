Grading the librarian, not the essay

Analogy To grade a librarian, you don't read the essays their visitors wrote. You check one thing: did they fetch the right book? Give them questions you already know the answer-location for, and count how often the right book is in the little stack they bring back.

That count is called recall@3: for each test question, did a correct chunk appear anywhere in the top 3 retrieved? The answer key — which chunk is "correct" for which question — is called the gold set. Yours comes from the data dictionary you printed in Section 3.

Why B tends to trail here: the gold questions ask about values (“P95 latency…”), which favors card-text matching — and the modality gap taxes B's mixed ranking.

Measure retrieval (recall@k: right chunk in the stack?) separately from answers (correct and grounded? — human review or an LLM judge). A wrong answer can be retrieval's fault or generation's; separate metrics tell you which.

Press play: RAG on a video

Analogy A video is a duet: a voice singing (the audio) and a slideshow behind it (the frames). To take notes on a duet, you write down what was said and what was shown — side by side, with the clock time in the margin.

That's the entire trick. The audio becomes a timestamped transcript; the frames become sampled keyframes (one every 5 seconds — nobody indexes all of them). Then each transcript segment gets paired with the keyframes from its time window. (Wondering how audio becomes a transcript in the first place? # Cell 8.1 — grading the librarian (steps ①–③ above):
def recall_at_k(strategy, k=3):
    r = MultiModalRetriever(strategy, k); hits = 0
    for q, want in gold.items():
        got = {d["id"] for d in r.retrieve(q)}
        hits += len(want & got) > 0          # any gold chunk in the stack?
    return hits / len(gold)

# Cell 9.4 — the duet, written down: pair each spoken segment with its frames:
for i,(t0, t1, text) in enumerate(segments):
    imgs = [fp for (t, fp) in frames if t0 <= t < t1]   # frames inside the window
    out.append({"id": f"seg_{i:03d}", "t_start": t0, "t_end": t1,
                "transcript": text, "keyframes": imgs})
Cell 9.3 samples the keyframes (one per 5s); Cell 9.5 indexes both streams with the same stores and RRF from lesson 4; Cell 9.6 answers with timestamps.
