Two judges, one leaderboard

Strategy C refuses to choose: a text map and a CLIP image map, each doing what it's best at. The puzzle is merging their answers fairly — and the solution fits in three lines of your notebook.

⏱ about 15 minutes · covers notebook Sections 5 (Strategy C), 6 & 7 · previous: Lesson 3

Two stores, two specialists

Strategy C keeps two separate Qdrant collections: text chunks embedded by the strong text model, and images embedded by CLIP from pixels. Every question is asked to both, giving two ranked lists.

Analogy Two judges at a talent show. One scores on a 0-to-1 scale, the other roughly 0-to-0.4. You can't average their raw scores — a “0.31” from one judge might be high praise, from the other, a shrug. But placements are always fair: 1st is 1st on any scale.

Merging by placement is called Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF). Step through it with the notebook's own demo question:

Step-through · RRF merges two rankings — “Which quarter had the highest revenue?”

Is the revenue chart's 0.31 better or worse than board notes' 0.51? Unanswerable — different embedders, different rulers. Raw scores must not be compared.

② Throw away scores, keep placements

Each item earns points from its rank alone: points = 1 / (60 + rank)

The 60 is a smoothing constant from the original RRF paper — it keeps rank #1 from crushing rank #2. An item appearing in both lists sums its points from each, which pushes it up hard.

③ One fair leaderboard
1. 📊 revenue chart  ✓ the answer's home
2. doc_board_notes.md  (“revenue strongest… public-sector deal”)
3. doc_gtm_strategy.md

Ties between the two #1s break by whichever helps downstream — the point is: the image made the final top-3 on fair terms, no modality gap, no caption bottleneck. Both specialists heard; both counted.

RRF in one sentence: ignore scores, sum 1/(60+rank) across lists, sort. Rank-based, so any two retrievers can be fused — even ones that were never designed to meet.

Key idea The notebook's practical default: Strategy C for finding, VLM-with-pixels for answering. C covers each modality with its best specialist; the pixels-at-answer-time step covers every strategy's shared weakness — none of them can read.

# Cell 5.4 — RRF, the whole trick (step ② you just clicked through):
def reciprocal_rank_fusion(result_lists, k=60):
    scores, keep = {}, {}
    for results in result_lists:
        for rank, doc in enumerate(results):
            key = (doc.metadata["modality"], doc.metadata["id"])
            scores[key] = scores.get(key, 0.0) + 1.0 / (k + rank + 1)
            keep[key] = doc
    return [keep[key] for key in sorted(scores, key=scores.get, reverse=True)]
