Side-by-side · growing the eval, honestly

What the activities were actually teaching

Analogy A driving lesson ends with the instructor saying: "now you drive, on a route I didn't plan." Not because your route matters — because choosing and handling it yourself is the skill. The notebook's demo queries were the instructor's route: curated, guaranteed to work. The activities handed you the wheel.
The two reflexes each activity was building

🔬 Activity #1 · Question the system

Goal: make you predict a ranking before running it, then explain the result from the system's design. The tradeoffs table stops being a thing you read and becomes a thing you reason with. This is the everyday skill of retrieval debugging: weird results come back, and you must say which part of the machine caused them.

📏 Activity #2 · Question the ruler

Goal: make you build a piece of the measuring instrument yourself — and watch the score move for a reason that had nothing to do with the retriever improving. Once you've seen that, "our RAG has 85% recall" always triggers the right question: recall on which questions?
One activity probes the system, the other probes the measurement. Together they move you from running a pipeline to interrogating one — which is the actual job of an AI engineer.

Now rehearse it — out loud

Don't memorize a script; you'll sound like you're reading. Instead: each card below is a cue. Say your answer out loud first, then open the reveal and compare. If your version carried the same idea — even in totally different words — that's a pass. That comparison loop is the rehearsal.

Beat 2 — who won and why

🎤 Cue: Which strategy nailed both queries, and what made that possible?

Compare with the beat
"Strategy A put the right chart first both times — because the captions the VLM wrote at ingestion happened to contain exactly what I asked about: the words 'pie chart' and the headcount numbers."
Beat 3 — the surprise (this is your best moment)

🎤 Cue: What surprised you about Strategy B's results?

Compare with the beat
"I expected the pie-chart phrasing to be CLIP's best case — a pie chart is visually unmistakable. Instead B returned zero images for both queries. That's the modality gap: my query is text, so it lands near text no matter how visually I phrase it. The gap is about where the vectors live, not what the words describe."
Beat 4 — the fix in action

🎤 Cue: How did Strategy C recover the charts B lost?

Compare with the beat
"Strategy C keeps a separate image store, so each chart earns an honest rank among images — then RRF merges by placement. Both charts came back at #2. That's the 'separate stores fused by rank' fix for the gap, working live."
Speak first, reveal second. Run it twice — the second pass is where it becomes yours.
