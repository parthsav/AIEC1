"Not totally accurate" — you're right. Here's why.

Analogy Whisper comes in sizes, like hiring note-takers. base is the fast intern: quick, cheap, occasionally writes down what something sounded like. small is a careful senior: slower, but hears through mumbles. Same training, more brainpower.

Three things to observe: the opening phrase changed completely (both may still be wrong — only you know); "time stamp everything. / I want in a video." became one correct sentence; and 5 choppy segments became 2 natural ones. More parameters → better hearing and better phrasing.

Notice the kind of mistake: "the eyes of the spirit" isn't random garbage — it's a phonetically plausible guess. That's the spectrogram story from lesson 6 confirming itself: the model heard sound-shapes and picked the likeliest words. When it's unsure (fast speech, first words of a recording, background noise, accents), "likeliest" can miss.

Key idea · measuring "not totally accurate" The field's ruler is word error rate (WER): what fraction of words are wrong. But look what computing it requires — the true words, written down. An answer key. You've met this pattern: lesson 5's recall@3 needed a gold set. Evaluation always starts with ground truth.
