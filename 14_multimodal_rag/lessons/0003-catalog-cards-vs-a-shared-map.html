Multimodal RAG · Lesson 3

Catalog cards vs a shared map

Two rival plans for finding a picture with words. Plan A searches the archivist's cards. Plan B puts the pictures themselves on the meaning map — and stumbles in a famous, fixable way.

⏱ about 15 minutes · covers notebook Section 5, Strategies A & B · previous: Lesson 2

Strategy A — search the cards

You already know this one — it's just lesson 1's pipeline fed with lesson 2's catalog cards. Each image's card is embedded like any text chunk, so everything lives in one Qdrant collection.

Strategy A · One map, images represented by their cards
Simple and it works — Cell 5.1 builds this in seven lines. But the chart sits where its card says it sits. If the card missed something you ask about, the chart is unfindable. The notebook calls this the caption bottleneck.

Strategy B — put the pictures on the map

Analogy Imagine a map-maker who grew up bilingual in words and pictures. Shown 400 million photo-with-caption pairs, they learned to place a photo and its caption at the same spot on one map. Hand them a sentence — or raw pixels — and they'll pin either one, no card needed.

That map-maker is a model called CLIP. It embeds text and images into one shared space — so your plain-English question can be compared directly against pixels.

Interactive · CLIP's shared map — and its famous flaw
Click “Search the whole map”. Your question is text — watch which island it lands on, and who its “nearest neighbors” turn out to be.
Key idea · The modality gap In CLIP's space, text clusters near text and images cluster near images — a measured, well-studied effect (Liang et al., NeurIPS 2022). So in one mixed collection, a text question sits closer to any text than to the best-matching image. Fixes: filter to images (what you just clicked), or keep separate stores and merge by rank — that's lesson 4.

And there's a second weakness. Ask CLIP's map to find “the latency chart” — brilliant, straight from pixels, no card involved. But ask “what IS the P95 latency in APAC?” and CLIP is useless: it places pictures, it doesn't read them. The answer, 240ms, is on the chart:

What CLIP can find but cannot read · fig_latency_region.png
CLIP gets you to this chart from the words “P95 latency” alone. Reading 240ms off it is Job #2 — the VLM with real pixels at answer time (lesson 2).

class ClipEmbedder:
    def embed_text(self, texts):    # door for sentences
        return self._norm(self.model.encode(list(texts)))

Why choose at all?

A finds by cards (great for text, misses what cards miss). B finds by pixels (great for images, tripped by the gap). Each is strongest exactly where the other is weak.

Next lesson: Strategy C — keep both, merge fairly. Two separate stores, each with its best embedder, fused by a beautifully simple trick called Reciprocal Rank Fusion. Then the payoff: the model reading $27M off the real chart.
