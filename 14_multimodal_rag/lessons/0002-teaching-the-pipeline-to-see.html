Teaching the pipeline to see

Lesson 1 ended on a problem: your pipeline is blind. The $27M lives only in pixels, and a text embedder can't read pixels. Today: the model that fixes this — by doing two completely different jobs.

A model that can see

Analogy So far your librarian could only read. A Vision-Language Model is a librarian who can also look at paintings — you can slide a picture across the desk along with your question.

That's the whole definition: a VLM is a chat model that accepts images inside its messages. In your notebook it's GPT-4o, created in Cell 0.3.

Models can't open files — they only read messages. So the picture is spelled out as text (base64) and packed into the message beside your words. Cell 4.1's three helpers (image_to_b64, image_block, multimodal_message) do exactly these three boxes.

Job #1 — the archivist (at ingestion, once)

Analogy A museum archivist walks the gallery once and writes a catalog card for every painting: what it shows, what stands out. Visitors don't search paintings — they search the card catalog.

④ The card joins the meaning map

The card's text (title + takeaway + data points) goes through lesson 1's pipeline unchanged: embed → store → findable. Cell 4.3 builds exactly this caption string.

Job #1: parse once, search forever. The VLM turns pixels into a text card; the card rides the ordinary text pipeline.

Job #2 — the expert reader (at answer time, every question)

Analogy The catalog card gets you to the right painting. But to answer “what's written on the tiny plaque in the corner?”, you bring an expert to stand in front of the actual painting.

At answer time, the notebook re-loads the retrieved image from disk and sends the actual pixels to the VLM — so it reads values fresh off the chart instead of trusting the card.

Keep these separate in your head — the notebook is literally organized around this split (Section 4 = job #1, Section 7 = job #2). Cards find; eyes read.
Key idea Why both? Because each job covers the other's weakness. Searching pixels directly is hard (lesson 3), so we search cards. But cards can be stale or incomplete, so we never answer from them — we answer from the pixels. Never throw the image away.

# Cell 4.1 — pack text + image into ONE message (the three boxes above):
def multimodal_message(prompt, image_paths=()):
    content = [{"type": "text", "text": prompt}] + [image_block(p) for p in image_paths]
    return HumanMessage(content=content)

But wait — is a card good enough?

Job #1 quietly made a big bet: that the archivist's card mentions whatever you'll ask about someday. If the card says “quarterly revenue” but you ask about “our biggest public-sector deal” — the card misses, and the chart is invisible again.
