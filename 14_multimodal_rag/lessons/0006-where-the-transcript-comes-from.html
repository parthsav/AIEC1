What a video file actually is

Analogy A video file is a lunchbox, not a sandwich. Open an .mp4 and there are separate compartments inside: a video track (the pictures), an audio track (the sound), sometimes a subtitle track. They're stored separately and played together.
Inside the lunchbox · what an .mp4 holds
ffmpeg can open the lunchbox and lift out one compartment. RAG uses the two tracks completely differently — which is the fork below. (Your demo file has only the top compartment.)

Down the green road: how sound becomes text

A microphone measures air pressure 16,000 times a second. Sound is that list. Sound familiar? Lesson 1's embeddings were lists of numbers too — this whole field is lists of numbers wearing different hats.

② Fold the wiggle into a picture
→

This picture is a spectrogram — like a piano roll: time runs right, pitch runs up, brightness = loudness. Every speech sound (“ee”, “sh”, “oh”) leaves its own visual fingerprint. We just turned a hearing problem into a seeing problem.

③ A model looks at the picture… and types
🎹 spectrogram
(30-second window)
→
👁 encoder
“looks” at the picture
→
⌨️ decoder
“types” word by word
Revenue reached a record twenty-seven million dollars…

This is Whisper (OpenAI, trained on 680,000 hours of speech). And notice the shape of the job: look at a picture, write text about it. You've met this before — it's lesson 2's Job #1, the VLM describing a chart. Same pattern, different picture.

④ Clock marks while typing
⏱ 6.0s Revenue reached a record twenty-seven million dollars in the fourth quarter… ⏱ 16.0s Monthly churn spiked to six point two percent in March… ⏱ 26.0s

As it types, Whisper also drops little clock marks between phrases — it learned to predict “what time we're at” the same way it predicts words. Slice the text at the clock marks and every phrase knows its start and end time.

⑤ Out comes the file you already know
{"segments": [
  {"start": 6.0,  "end": 16.0, "text": "Revenue reached a record twenty-seven million..."},
  {"start": 16.0, "end": 26.0, "text": "Monthly churn spiked to six point two percent..."},
  {"start": 26.0, "end": 37.0, "text": "...APAC ninety-fifth percentile latency reached two hundred forty milliseconds..."}
]}

Compare this with data/video/fy2024_review_transcript.json — it's the same shape, because that file was written to imitate Whisper's output. From here, lesson 5's timeline takes over: segments → chunks → stores → “[00:26–00:37]”.

The whole road: air wiggle → number list → sound picture → words with clock marks. Nothing mystical — each hop is small.

The pattern worth keeping

Zoom out Multimodal RAG keeps playing the same move: turn what you can't search into something you can. Charts → catalog cards (lesson 2). Pixels → CLIP coordinates (lesson 3). Speech → a sound-picture → timestamped text (today). New modality? Ask: “what's the searchable form, and which model gets it there?”
