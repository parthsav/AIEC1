Lesson 0003 · Reinforcement Learning Basics

Learning to Cook With No Recipe, Only a Food Critic

Analogy first You're learning to make a new dish with no recipe. A food critic tastes your attempt and gives it a score out of 10 — nothing else, no tips. Next time you cook, you adjust based only on that score: more salt scored well last time, so you try more salt again. Repeat for weeks, and you converge on a good dish — without anyone ever telling you the steps.

That's reinforcement learning (RL): improve by trying things and getting scored, not by copying a shown example. Three words carry all the weight:

TermIn the cooking storyIn this notebook
Policyyour current way of cookingthe model's current weights — literally its current habit of generating text
Actionone attempt at the dishone completion the model generates for a question
Rewardthe critic's scorea number from a reward function (Lesson 0005)

"Policy" is the one word worth sitting with: it does not mean a rulebook someone wrote. It means the model itself, right now — its current tendency to produce certain words over others. Training doesn't rewrite rules; it nudges that tendency.

Animation — a policy shifting toward what scores well

The bars are how likely the policy is to try each strategy again. Nobody hand-picked "more salt" as correct — it simply scored best, so the policy leans toward it more with every round.

Why not just show it good reasoning? (SFT vs. RL)

Session 15's title says "reasoning model," so why not collect thousands of worked math solutions and train the model to copy them — the same supervised approach used elsewhere in this course? Two practical reasons this notebook doesn't:

Copying worked examples (SFT)

Someone has to write thousands of good step-by-step reasoning traces by hand — slow, expensive, and the human's reasoning style may not even be the best one for this model.

Rewarding checkable answers (RL / GRPO)

GSM8K already has a final numeric answer for every question. Checking "is this number right?" is nearly free — no reasoning trace needed at all.
Where this lives in your notebook Task 4's markdown cell says it directly: "Notice something odd about the data: it is just questions and final answers — no reasoning traces... That is the point." Look at get_gsm8k_questions() — it only ever extracts the answer after ####, never a reasoning trace.
