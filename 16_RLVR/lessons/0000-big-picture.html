Lesson 0000 · Big Picture

Building the Referee, Not the Player

Session 15 was about the player — you changed a model's weights so it got better at answering. This session is about the referee — the thing that decides whether an answer was actually right. You never questioned the referee in Session 15; it was just there, already working. Now you build one.

Analogy first — picking up where Lesson 0000 of Session 15 left off Remember the free-throw analogy? You let someone shoot a hundred free throws and tell them which ones went in, instead of moving their arms for them. Session 15 never asked: who is standing under the hoop deciding "in" or "out"? That's the referee. This session, you build the referee — and you learn what happens when a player figures out how to fool it.

In RL vocabulary, that referee is called a verifier, and a reward built from one is a verifiable reward. This whole session is that one idea, taken apart piece by piece: what makes a reward verifiable, how to build a verifier for two different domains (math, code), what happens when a policy learns to cheat it, and how its verdicts become training data.

What's actually new here

Four ideas, each getting its own lesson:

  1. Verifiable rewards — what makes a reward "verifiable" at all, and the ground-truth + extraction machinery it needs → Lesson 0001
  2. Reward function design — why +1.0 / −0.1, not +1.0 / −1.0 → Lesson 0002
  3. Sampling groups & verifying them — the same group structure GRPO used, now doing double duty → Lesson 0003
  4. Reward hacking — what a policy does once the referee is the objective → Lesson 0004

After that: verifying code instead of math, and turning verified samples into training data two different ways.
