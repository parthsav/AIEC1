Lesson 0003 · Sample and Verify

One Shooter, Four Free Throws, One Referee

Analogy first — same shooter you met in Session 15 Remember: GRPO didn't judge one free throw in isolation. It had the same shooter take a whole group of shots at the same hoop, then compared each shot to that group's own average. This lesson builds the referee's half of that: the same policy answers the same question n_samples times, and the verifier calls each attempt in or out — building the exact group your GRPO training consumed in Session 15.

The code: one problem in, a group of verified samples out

Code — Task 4: sampling and verifying a group

@dataclass
class Sample:
    problem: str
    response: str
    extracted: str
    reward: float
    verified_correct: bool

def sample_and_verify(problem: Problem, n_samples: int = 4) -> list[Sample]:
    """Sample a group of completions for one problem and verify each one."""
    group = []
    for _ in range(n_samples):
        response = simple_complete(
            f"Solve step by step. Put the final numeric answer in \\boxed{{}}.\n\n{problem.question}",
            system="You are a careful mathematician. Show your work, then box the final number.",
        )
        extracted = extract_number(response)
        reward = reward_fn.compute(response, problem.answer)
        group.append(Sample(problem=problem.question, response=response,
                             extracted=extracted, reward=reward,
                             verified_correct=reward > 0))
    return group

groups = [sample_and_verify(p) for p in problems]
Python patterns you'll see here for _ in range(n_samples): — the underscore is a throwaway variable name, used when you need to repeat something a fixed number of times but never actually use the loop counter. groups = [sample_and_verify(p) for p in problems] is a list comprehension: run sample_and_verify once per problem, collect all the results into one list — five problems in, five groups out.

Why the group matters — twice

Reason 1 — GRPO already needs this

GRPO computes each sample's advantage relative to its group's average reward. This groups list is exactly that structure: one list of scored attempts per prompt.

Reason 2 — new this session

A group with both correct and incorrect samples is raw material for a preference pair: sample 1 or 2 as "chosen," sample 3 as "rejected." No contrast, no pair. Lesson 0006 builds this.
Where this lives in your notebook Task 4, plus the note right after it: "If your verified-correct rate is 100%, the contrast that drives learning is missing." That's not a throwaway warning — a group with no failures is useless for both GRPO (every advantage is roughly zero, nothing to push the policy toward) and for preference pairs (Reason 2 above needs at least one rejected sample per group). The notebook also has you read one rejected completion directly — that's the fastest way to learn what your policy is actually getting wrong: arithmetic, misread units, or bad formatting.
